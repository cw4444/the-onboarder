# Glossary

What the UK words in these notes mean, in plain English. Handy if you've never worked in the UK, or never worked
in commercial cleaning. This explains the words only. What you actually do is in the other notes, and anything
that isn't there goes to Dean Okafor.

## Pay and tax

- **P45:** the form your last employer gives you when you leave a job. It shows how much you earned and how much
  tax you paid so far this tax year. Your new employer uses it to put you on the right tax code.
- **P60:** the summary your employer gives you after each tax year, showing your pay and the tax you paid.
- **Starter checklist:** the government form you fill in instead of a P45, if you haven't got one. It's from
  HMRC.
- **HMRC:** HM Revenue & Customs, the UK government's tax office. The US equivalent is the IRS.
- **Tax code:** a short code that tells your employer how much tax to take from your pay.
- **Emergency tax code:** a temporary tax code used when your employer doesn't have your details yet. You can end
  up paying too much tax until it's sorted. That's why the P45 matters.
- **National Insurance number:** your personal number for UK tax and benefits. It's roughly like a US Social
  Security number.
- **Payroll cut-off:** the date your pay details must be sorted by to make that month's pay. Here it's the 15th.

## Starting work

- **Right to work check:** by law, UK employers have to check you're allowed to work in the UK before you start.
- **Yoti:** the company that does the right to work check here, by email.
- **Post Office ID app:** the app the Yoti email asks you to use to prove your ID.
- **Rota:** your work schedule.

## Safety

- **COSHH:** short for Control of Substances Hazardous to Health, the UK rules on handling dangerous chemicals.
  The **COSHH cupboard** is the locked cupboard where the cleaning chemicals are kept.
- **Accident book:** where anything that happens to you at work gets written down, however small.
- **First aider:** a member of staff trained in first aid.
- **Fire assembly point:** where everyone meets outside if the fire alarm goes off.
- **999:** the UK emergency number for police, fire and ambulance. The US equivalent is 911.

## Everyday words

- **Ground floor:** the floor at street level. **Floor 1** is the one above it, so UK "floor 1" is US "second
  floor".
- **Lift:** elevator. **Car park:** parking lot. **Bin:** trash can. **Hoover / hoovering:** vacuum / vacuuming.
- **Trousers:** pants. **Polo:** a short-sleeved shirt with a collar.
- **Crocs:** the rubber clogs. Not allowed here.
- **Ring:** phone someone.
